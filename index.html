📖 Glossary — what the terms mean
Antimicrobial resistance (AMR)
When bacteria evolve to survive a drug that used to kill them.
Susceptible
A cell with no resistance — any antibiotic flooding its area kills it fast.
Resistance gene
A gene that lets a cell survive ONE specific antibiotic. Each drug has its own.
Multi-drug resistance (MDR / “superbug”)
A cell carrying several resistance genes at once — survives several drugs.
Efflux pump
A molecular pump that ejects the antibiotic from the cell. How a resistant cell survives a flood — it glows while pumping and spends energy to do it.
Mutation
A random gene change when a cell divides — how brand-new resistance first appears.
Plasmid & horizontal gene transfer (HGT)
A ring of DNA a dead resistant cell leaves behind. A living cell that touches it picks up the gene — resistance spreading sideways, without reproduction.
Fitness cost
Carrying resistance burns extra energy every second, so resistant cells lose out when no drug is present. That’s why resistance stays rare until you apply a drug.
MIC (minimum inhibitory concentration)
The lowest drug dose that stops a susceptible cell growing. In the MEGA-plate the bands are multiples of MIC (1×, 10×, 100×, 1000×).
