📖 Glossary — what the terms mean
Antimicrobial resistance (AMR)
When bacteria evolve to survive a drug that used to kill them.
Susceptible
A cell with MIC 0 — any antibiotic dosed onto its area kills it fast.
MIC (minimum inhibitory concentration)
The lowest drug dose that stops a cell growing. Here each cell has a numeric MIC level per drug (0 = susceptible, up to 4). It survives a dose only when its MIC ≥ that dose; just below, it takes reduced (not full) damage. The MEGA-plate bands are MIC multiples: 1× (level 1) → 10× → 100× → 1000× (level 4). Brighter cell colour = higher MIC.
Multi-drug resistance (MDR / “superbug”)
A cell with raised MIC to several drugs at once — survives several antibiotics.
Efflux pump
A molecular pump that ejects the antibiotic from the cell. How a resistant cell survives a dose — it glows while pumping and spends energy in proportion to its MIC.
Mutation
When a cell divides, each drug's MIC can step up or down one band — biased upward under drug pressure. How resistance climbs (and decays when the drug is gone).
Plasmid & horizontal gene transfer (HGT)
A ring of DNA a dead resistant cell leaves behind, carrying its MIC for one drug. A living cell that touches it raises its own MIC to match — resistance spreading sideways, without reproduction.
Fitness cost
Carrying resistance burns extra energy every second — and the higher the MIC, the more it costs — so resistant cells lose out when no drug is present. That’s why resistance stays rare until you apply a drug.
